| Situation | Practical option | Check first |

| Several ordinary bulbs on one circuit | Smart switch/dimmer | Neutral, 1-pole/3-way, dimmable load |

## Nine-part video guide

### 1. Introduction
Smart lighting is about reliable, convenient control—not just changing colors. Decide what the room needs before selecting a product.

### 2. Building blocks
**Bulbs** provide individual color and brightness control, but most require continuous power. **Switches and dimmers** control conventional lighting circuits and preserve familiar wall operation. **Hidden modules** add control behind existing switches in suitable installations. **Smart plugs** work for portable lamps. **Strips and fixtures** are ideal for accent lighting.

### 3. Philips Hue vs. Govee vs. Nanoleaf
Hue offers a broad lighting ecosystem; Govee has many colorful accent and entertainment products; Nanoleaf offers decorative panels alongside traditional lighting. Compare the *specific models*, not just brand names. Home Assistant compatibility and cloud requirements vary.

### 4. Martin Jerry and Inovelli
Martin Jerry offers various US-style wall switches, dimmers, 3-way and fan controls, including distinct Tasmota Wi-Fi and Zigbee models. Inovelli Blue products offer Zigbee-based switch options. Verify exact wiring, switch type, load, and configuration before purchasing.

### 5. Shelly hidden modules
A compatible Shelly relay or dimmer may allow the original switch to remain. **Relays and dimmers are not interchangeable.** Check neutral requirements, enclosure capacity, electrical ratings, installation instructions and applicable code. Use a qualified electrician when appropriate.

### 6. Smart plugs and energy monitoring
Automate compatible table and floor lamps without rewiring. Power monitoring does not mean it is safe to abruptly disconnect every connected appliance; avoid cutting power to active computers and similar sensitive equipment.

### 7. Ceiling fans
**Never control a fan motor with an ordinary light dimmer.** Verify fan motor type (AC/DC), remote receiver, wiring, and the controller's documented compatibility.

### 8. Wi-Fi, Zigbee, Thread, Matter and Z-Wave
| Technology | Meaning | Typical infrastructure |
|---|---|---|
| Wi-Fi | Network connection; local or cloud-dependent by device | Router/access point |
| Zigbee | Low-power mesh | Compatible coordinator/hub |
| Thread | IP-based low-power mesh | Thread border router for connection to other IP networks |
| Matter | Interoperability standard, **not** a radio | Compatible controller and Wi-Fi or Thread transport |
| Z-Wave | Separate mesh protocol | Z-Wave controller |

An **internet outage** is not the same as failure of the router, Home Assistant server, coordinator, or power. Offline behavior depends on the entire system.

### 9. Whole-house recommendations
- **Kitchen:** switches/dimmers for overhead lighting; strips for cabinets.
- **Living room:** main wall controls plus smart plugs and optional color accents.
- **Bedrooms:** normal switches with bedside lamp automation.
- **Hallways:** motion/presence automations with physical fallback.
- **Exterior:** appropriate outdoor-rated lights and scheduled porch controls.
- **Fans:** dedicated compatible fan/light controllers.
- **Home Assistant:** optional local automation and mixed-brand dashboard.

## Before buying

1. Confirm the exact device supports the desired local control and Home Assistant integration.
2. Check wiring: neutral availability, 1-pole vs 3-way, load type, electrical rating and box space.
3. Keep physical wall controls intuitive for family members and guests.
4. Plan for internet and local-network failures.
5. Check compatibility for smart bulb mode, dimming, and fan motors.
6. Consult manufacturer documentation and applicable electrical requirements.
