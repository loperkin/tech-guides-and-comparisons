## 🧭 How I Compare Products

I don't believe there's one perfect product for everyone.

A solution that works great for my home might be completely wrong for yours.

When comparing technology, I generally look at:

| Category | What Matters |
|---|---|
| 💰 Cost | Initial investment, subscriptions, and long-term expenses |
| 🔧 Setup | Installation difficulty, configuration, and maintenance |
| 🏠 Compatibility | Integration with Home Assistant and other platforms |
| 🔒 Privacy | Local control, cloud dependencies, and data considerations |
| ⚡ Reliability | Offline capabilities, failure points, and recovery |
| 🧩 Flexibility | Customization, expansion, and integration options |
| 🛠️ Repairability | Replacement parts, documentation, and long-term usability |
| 🎯 Value | Whether the benefits justify the expense |

Not every comparison will cover every category.

But the goal is always the same: **understand the trade-offs before spending money.**

## 🧪 Hands-On Experience vs Research

There's an important difference between something I've personally tested and something I've researched.

I'll try to make that distinction clear.

**🛠️ Hands-On Review**

Products I've personally used, installed, configured, or tested.

**⚖️ Product Comparison**

A side-by-side evaluation using documented features, specifications, research, and practical considerations. Some products may not have been personally tested.

**🛒 Buying Guide**

Recommendations intended to help you choose equipment for a specific purpose or budget.

**🔬 Experiments & Follow-Ups**

Updates, discoveries, limitations, and things that changed after the original comparison.

I won't pretend I've tested something I haven't.

And if I discover that something I previously recommended isn't working as expected, I'll do my best to document that too.

Because a useful comparison should help you make a decision, not just convince you to buy something.

## 🧠 The Bigger Idea

I started **Purpose in Practice** because I believe there's a difference between knowing something and knowing what to do with it.

That applies to buying technology just as much as building it.

You don't always need the newest equipment.

You don't always need the most expensive equipment.

And sometimes the best solution is something you already own.

**The point is to understand your options, make something useful, and keep learning.**
